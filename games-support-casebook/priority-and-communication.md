# Priority, Communication and Handover Guide

This is an **illustrative practice framework**, not an employer's SLA or moderation policy. Replace the sample targets with the team's documented rules.

## Severity model

| Priority | Example | Handling principle |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 — Critical** | Widespread login/payment outage, confirmed data exposure, credible immediate safety threat | Raise incident/security/safety escalation immediately; keep updates coordinated |
| **P2 — High** | Single-player account access blocked, confirmed payment missing, repeated disconnects, formal ban appeal | Prioritise timely investigation and clear next-update commitment |
| **P3 — Normal** | Intermittent issue with workaround, isolated gameplay defect, non-urgent technical question | Diagnose with a reproducible checklist; track patterns |
| **P4 — Low** | General question, suggestion or cosmetic feedback | Provide concise guidance and route feedback appropriately |

Priority is based on **impact × urgency × risk**, not how angry the player sounds. Increase priority when multiple reports share a signature, release, platform, region or time window.

## First-response structure

1. Acknowledge the impact.
2. State what you can do now.
3. Ask only for the minimum safe evidence.
4. Set a realistic next update or explain the next step.
5. Avoid blame, jargon and promises outside your authority.

**Example**

> Hi! Thanks for reporting this. I understand the issue is preventing you from accessing the game. I'll check the account and service status first. Please share your platform, region, the approximate time the issue started (including timezone), and the error code. I'll update you once the first checks are complete.

## Useful communication phrases

| Situation | Example phrase |
|---|---|
| Need more detail | "To narrow this down, could you share the platform, game version and approximate time of the issue?" |
| Waiting for another team | "I've sent the verified details to the relevant team. The case remains open, and I'll share an update when they complete the next check." |
| No confirmed root cause yet | "We haven't confirmed the cause yet. I'm checking whether other reports match the same symptoms." |
| No authority to promise an outcome | "I can submit this for review, but I can't guarantee the decision." |
| Safe security reminder | "Please don't send your password, one-time codes or full payment-card details." |

## Handover checklist

- Is the impact and scope clear?
- Are times recorded with timezone?
- Are account, order, match and incident references exact?
- Is evidence preserved and sensitive data redacted?
- Are verified facts separated from hypotheses?
- Is the next owner explicit?
- Does the player know the next step and update expectation?
- Is a related incident, security or moderation queue linked?

## Quality review

A useful support response is empathetic, accurate, secure, actionable and traceable. Review tone and clarity, policy compliance, completeness of evidence, correct priority, escalation quality and whether the player received a clear next step. Do not score agents solely on speed or ticket count without considering case complexity and customer impact.
